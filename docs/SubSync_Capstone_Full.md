# INTRODUCTION
## Background of the Study
The rapid advancement of information technology has significantly transformed how communities manage information, communication, and service delivery throughout generations. Digital platforms are increasingly used to improve transparency, efficiency, and collaboration within organizations and communities. In residential subdivisions, these technologies can help manage announcements, and community concerns more effectively. However, many subdivisions still rely on traditional methods such as paper-based records, bulletin board announcements, and manual communication. These methods often result in delays, miscommunication, and limited transparency for residents.
The transition towards technology-based community monitoring systems has been extremely effective in supporting local planning and decision-making processes. A good example is the Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS), which highlights how local data can be useful in helping local governments identify specific needs in the communities they serve (Reyes & Mandap, 2022). Similarly, intelligent community management models emphasize the use of information technology and communication networks to improve governance and operational efficiency. These systems integrate communication, monitoring, and data management to support better community services (Tang, 2022). As communities grow, the use of digital systems becomes increasingly important for efficient administration.


<!-- Source PDF page 5 -->
Digital reporting platforms also play an important role in encouraging community participation. These systems allow residents to submit complaints, concerns, and suggestions directly to administrators. Such platforms enable faster monitoring and response to issues within the community (Anggara et al., 2023). In addition, interactive mapping technologies improve usability by visually presenting geographic information. This allows users to easily locate facilities and identify reported issues within a subdivision (Bartling et al., 2021).
Web-based information systems have also been widely used to manage resident data and improve service delivery. These systems simplify the storage, access, and management of community information compared to traditional methods. They also enhance reliability, usability, and efficiency in handling administrative tasks (Melendres & Aranda, 2022). Despite these technological developments, many residential subdivisions still lack integrated digital management platforms. As a result, residents may experience limited access to information and reduced engagement with community management.
To address these challenges, this study proposes the development of SubSync: An Automated Web-Based Platform for Enhancing Community Engagement in Residential Subdivisions. The system aims to improve transparency, communication, and participation within residential communities. It integrates a communication tools, issue reporting, recommendations, and interactive mapping features. These features are designed to provide a centralized platform for residents and administrators. Through this system, the study seeks to enhance community engagement and improve subdivision management.

## Community Management Systems for Residential Communities
Web‑based community management systems provide centralized platforms for storing data, facilitating communication, and coordinating services within defined groups. Research on digital platforms that support community assessment and governance emphasizes that organizational factors and system-wide design standards significantly influence the effectiveness of implementation and long-term community health (Luo & Chen, 2024). These systems help users interact, share information, and collaborate within a digital environment, acting as a backbone for community operations. Features such as user profiles, member directories, and structured service modules make digital community platforms instrumental for building transparency and cohesion among residents.
In the Philippine setting, community platforms like Webyu and barangay management information systems illustrate how localized solutions improve grassroots

These examples affirm the relevance of centralized community management systems in enhancing operational efficiency and resident participation.

## Synthesis
The reviewed related literature and studies show that community management systems have significantly improved the way residential communities, organizations, and cooperatives manage their operations through digital platforms. These systems commonly include community or member management, issue reporting, communication and announcement systems, and web-based dashboards. ## Research Design
The study employs a Descriptive-Developmental Research Design to guide the design, development, and evaluation of SubSync: An Automated Web-Based Platform for Enhancing Community Engagement in Residential Subdivisions. This research design is appropriate for the study because it involves both an examination of the subdivision's existing conditions and the development of a system that addresses identified operational problems. This ensures that the system developed is aligned with the community's needs and administrative operations.
The descriptive component of the research design is used to examine and describe the current manual processes within the residential subdivision. Descriptive research is defined as a method that systematically describes a population, situation, or phenomenon without manipulating variables, focusing on existing conditions (Osio et al., 2026). In this study, it includes the management of resident records, dissemination of announcements, and the handling of community issue reports. This phase focuses on identifying and understanding operational issues such as delays in communication, data redundancy in paper-based ledgers, and the lack of a centralized data management system (Pinero, 2024). The results of this phase serve as the basis for determining the functional requirements of the proposed platform.


<!-- Source PDF page 21 -->
The developmental component of the research design is applied in the design and development of SubSync, which involves the creation of a web-based platform for administrators and residents. Development in system and software research refers to the process of designing, building, and iteratively improving software systems to solve real-world problems and meet user requirements (Perera, 2025). The system integrates key modules such as resident management, an automated announcement system, and an issue reporting module. The development process follows an Agile Methodology, an iterative approach involving continuous cycles of planning, design, development, and testing to ensure that the system effectively addresses identified problems and meets the operational requirements of the subdivision (Ilustrisimo, 2026).
Through this research design, SubSync is developed based on the actual needs and operational challenges of the subdivision stakeholders, ensuring the system is grounded in real-world conditions and accurately reflects the existing workflow. By combining both descriptive analysis and developmental system design, the study bridges the gap between manual processes and digital solutions. This approach results in an integrated and centralized platform that improves data accuracy, enhances operational efficiency, and streamlines the overall management of residential activities, which is then evaluated using the software quality standards of ISO/IEC 25010:2011 (Dandan, 2026).

## Sampling Technique
The researchers will employ a Purposive Sampling Technique, a prominent non-probability sampling method where participants are selected based on the researcher's judgment regarding which individuals will be most informative for the study's specific objectives. Unlike probability-based methods that rely on random selection, purposive sampling focuses on criterion-based selection, ensuring that each respondent possesses unique insights or experiences essential to the phenomenon under study. This approach is particularly effective in research where the validity of the results depends on the depth of the participants' first-hand knowledge rather than general representativeness.
For the evaluation of SubSync, this technique is appropriate as it allows the researchers to target individuals with direct, practical experience in subdivision living or administrative management. Selecting respondents such as homeowners and subdivision officers ensures that the feedback regarding the platform's functional suitability and usability is grounded in real-world administrative challenges. Furthermore, for the technical evaluation phase, the researchers specifically target IT Professionals and Software Experts who meet predefined inclusion criteria, such as prior experience in system analysis or software quality assessment. These experts possess the specialized technical competency required to audit a web-based platform's architecture and security protocols. By deliberately selecting both end-users and technical specialists, the study achieves a balanced and rigorous analysis of the system's performance based on established global standards like ISO/IEC 25010.

## Data Gathering Instrument
The primary research instrument used in this study is a combination of qualitative and quantitative tools designed to capture both the functional requirements and the eventual quality of the system. For the initial phase, the researchers will utilize a Semi-Structured Interview Guide to gather in-depth insights from subdivision administrators and homeowners' association officers. This instrument is designed to quantitatively measure the system's performance across eight key characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, Compatibility, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, and Portability. The questionnaire utilizes a 5-point Likert Scale, ranging from "Strongly Disagree" (1) to "Strongly Agree" (5), providing a standardized metric for both IT professionals and end-users to assess the platform’s effectiveness in a subdivision environment.

A context diagram, also known as a Level 0 Data Flow Diagram (DFD), represents the entire system as a single process to demonstrate the scope of the system and its interactions with external entities. This diagram illustrates the boundaries of the system and how information is exchanged between the platform and its primary users to ensure

As illustrated in the SubSync system, identified as the SubSync: An Automated Web-Based Platform for Enhancing Community Engagement in Residential Subdivisions, acts as the central hub for data exchange among three primary entities: the Resident, Admin, and Officer.
The Resident entity provides inputs such as issue reports, recommendations, profile and household information, uploaded media, and conversation replies to the system. In return, the system provides the resident with announcements, notifications, issue response updates, facility map information, and conversation messages. The Admin serves as the primary controller of the system, providing essential data such as user and household management details, facility map management, posted announcements, uploaded media, and issue management. The system provides the admin with resident suggestions, submitted issue reports, reports and monitoring data, and system alerts and notifications. Finally, the Officer interacts with the system to manage community concerns and communication. The system sends assigned issue reports, resident messages, and announcement updates to the officer, who in turn provides issue responses, posted announcements, uploaded media, and conversation messages back to the platform. By defining these high-level data flows, the context diagram ensures that all stakeholders have a clear understanding of the system's functional boundaries and the specific information required for effective community management.

## Data Flow Diagram
The Data Flow Diagram (DFD) provides a detailed visualization of the internal processes, data stores, and information pathways within the SubSync platform. As shown in the system, it is broken down into six core functional processes that manage the flow of information between the Resident, Admin, and Officer entities. The User & Household Management (1.0) process handles account creation, profile updates, and household information, storing user data in the D1 User Accounts data store and issuing system alerts or confirmations back to the users. ## Use Case Diagram
A use case diagram is a visual guide that shows how users interact with a system and the actions they can perform. It helps explain the main features and functions of a system in a simple and organized way by identifying the users, also called actors, and the tasks they can perform within the system. This type of diagram is important because it gives developers, researchers, and stakeholders a clear understanding of how the system operates and how each user will interact with it. It also helps ensure that user requirements are considered during the planning and development of the system (GeeksforGeeks, 2025; EDUCBA, 2023).


<!-- Source PDF page 41 -->
Figure 5 presents the use case diagram of SubSync: An Automated Web-Based Platform for Enhancing Community Engagement in Residential Subdivisions. The diagram illustrates the interactions among the four main actors of the system: Homeowner, Resident, Officer, and Admin.
The Homeowner can register an account, log in to the system, update profile information, submit complaints or issue reports, view announcements, and receive system notifications. These functions allow homeowners to actively participate in community activities and communicate concerns efficiently.
The Resident can manage household information and household members, view the status of submitted concerns, access announcements, review complaints and reports, receive notifications, and view available reports. These functions help residents stay informed and engaged with subdivision activities.
The Officer is responsible for supporting community operations by creating announcements, managing community policies, assigning administrative responsibilities, generating reports, and receiving notifications. Officers also use the system to coordinate with administrators and residents in addressing community concerns.
The Admin oversees the overall management of the platform by logging in as an administrator, managing users, managing households and residential records, assigning officers, creating announcements, generating reports, and receiving system notifications.
